Hi, this is Rabbit! 🐰

An experienced cook does not rush when the orders pile up. They lay out the ingredients first, decide the order, and taste once more before the plate goes out. It is not flashy technique but procedure in their bones that finishes the dish.

My third **SAP MM certification C_TS452** attempt ended at **97.14%**, a pass. From 31% on the first attempt to 97% on the third. The new SAP knowledge I gained in between was less than you might think. What changed was my **execution procedure**. This post lays out exactly what I did on exam day, in order.

> **Three-line summary**
> - Before starting, I chose English for the instructions and built a code sheet for my candidate number first.
> - I decided the task order after seeing the exam, spent about 85 minutes on tasks and about 35 on verification, and submitted after about 2 hours.
> - Passing is not the end but the start of Stay Certified. My next goal is PP, my day job.

## Before pressing start

- **English for the instructions**: the lesson from my [first failed attempt](/en/blog/sap-mm-c-ts452-certification-2). In a system whose menus and field names are in English, English instructions make comparison faster and more accurate.
- **Download the instructions as a PDF**: easier to read while switching between screens.
- **Build a code sheet for your number first**: each exam gives you a candidate number (Group ID), and that number goes into the names of the objects you create. Right at the start, I listed every code I would use in a table and afterward typed only from that table. It shuts out zero points from a typo in the number.

## Decide the task order after seeing the exam

I had an order planned from practice, but I set the actual order **after receiving the exam**. The questions can differ from attempt to attempt, so there was no reason to cling to a predetermined order. I used four criteria.

1. Number of items
2. Share of configuration (Customizing): the risk of time blowing up
3. Chain risk: whether a mistake early on wipes out everything after it
4. Similarity to the course exercises

Tasks that eat a lot of time but take everything down with them if the start fails, I handled while I still had plenty of time. I also set a rule: **if a task runs 10 minutes over its target, skip its remaining items** and move on.

## Time allocation in my third C_TS452 attempt

The exam allowed 3 hours, but I actually submitted after about 2.

- **Working through tasks**: about 80–90 minutes, following the practiced procedure without getting stuck
- **Full verification**: about 35 minutes
- **Time left over**: about 1 hour

During the tasks I never skipped the check right before saving. The 3-minute check from practice never came up, because I hardly got stuck anywhere; that was a sign of how well the practice had stuck. Whenever a document number was generated, I noted it immediately, so I would not waste time hunting for numbers during verification.

## Final full verification

As I said in [practice strategy](/en/blog/sap-mm-c-ts452-certification-3), what protected me in the exam was <strong class="key">verification</strong>. I opened the display screen for each item and compared it with the expected values.

- Master data: check values view by view in the display transaction
- Purchasing documents: check down to the status
- Goods receipts and invoices: check quantity, amount, and reference values in the document display

If you created an object again to fix a mistake, you cannot be sure which one will be graded. So always make sure **the most recent one is complete** too.

## Earning it is the start: Stay Certified

As I covered in the [SAP certification guide](/en/blog/sap-certification), SAP certification today is a membership you renew every year. My C_TS452 Stay Certified deadline is **October 2027**. Until then I need to keep my Learning Hub subscription and pass the annual assessment. Following the on-screen advice, it is best to finish the assessment with plenty of time, at least a month before the deadline.

## Next goal: PP

My next goal is my day job, production planning: **PP** (C_TS422). After that, I plan to take on management accounting, CO.

Doing MM first should help with PP too. Now I can see from the other side how MRP creates a purchase requisition, and how that requisition becomes a purchase order through source determination. It is as if I now understand the "why" from my [CPIM post](/en/blog/sap-cpim) a little more broadly. I will record my PP journey here too.

## Rabbit's Takeaway

Three attempts left me with four lines.

- **Knowing and doing are different.** A hands-on exam tests the latter.
- **Read the instructions in the original, and enter values as instructed.** Work habits and textbook habits are both traps.
- **Verification is the score.** Check right before saving, and verify checkpoints right away.
- **Failure is data.** ==Without the 31% on my first attempt, there would have been no 97% on my third.==

Just as an experienced cook's secret is procedure, the secret to 97% was, in the end, procedure I had made my own 😎
