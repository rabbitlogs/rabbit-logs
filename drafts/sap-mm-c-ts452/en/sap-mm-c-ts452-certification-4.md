Hi, this is Rabbit! 🐰

You have memorized the recipe and warmed up your hands. Then you walk into the kitchen and find the gas valve shut. However well a cook has prepared, there is nothing to do in front of a burner that will not light.

That was my second **SAP MM certification C_TS452** attempt. I had prepared according to my [practice strategy](/en/blog/sap-mm-c-ts452-certification-3), and in rehearsals I was finishing a 3-hour set in under 100 minutes. Honestly, I felt confident. The result was **25.71%**, lower than my first attempt. This post is a record of what I did when the exam system itself was broken.

> **Three-line summary**
> - Right after the second attempt started, a base setting was missing from the exam system, so I could not carry out most of the tasks.
> - I collected evidence that it was not my mistake, filed a case with the SAP Learning help center, and got the attempt back the same day.
> - I did not retake it immediately without confirmation that the defect was fixed, and rescheduled for a few days later.

## What happened in the second C_TS452 attempt

In early October, I booked my second attempt for a 2 p.m. slot.

![SAP Certification Hub screen confirming a C_TS452 exam booking, showing the confirmed date and time and a note that the attempt remains unused if you cannot join](/images/sap-mm-c-ts452-certification-4-01.jpg)
*Figure 1. Booking confirmation for the second attempt. If you cannot start the exam, the attempt stays unused.*

As soon as I started the first task, an odd error appeared. I was trying to do an inventory-related step, and saving was blocked with a message to the effect that **a base setting was missing**, one that should already exist in the exam system. It was not something candidates create; it should have been part of the exam environment from the start.

Many tasks depended on that setting, so in the end **I could not carry out most of them**. I did only what I could, and the result was 25.71%.

## My mistake, or an exam system defect?

The first thing to do in this situation is **make sure it is not your own mistake**. In a hands-on exam, getting an earlier step wrong and blocking a later one is common. I judged it to be a defect for three reasons.

- The setting in question was **one the instructions never asked me to create or change**
- The error appeared right after the exam started, **before I had changed anything**
- The same kind of step worked normally on the practice system

> 💡 **Key point**: if something feels off, capture the exact error message, the screen where it happened, and the time right away. When you write a support case, that is everything.

## How to file an SAP support case

After the exam, I filed an <strong class="key">SAP support case</strong>. The route is simple: go to the [SAP Learning help center](https://learning.sap.com/helpcenter) and submit an inquiry with **Create a case**.

This is what I included in the case.

- The exam code and the date and time of the attempt
- Where the error happened, the exact message, and a screenshot of the error screen
- An explanation that the setting was outside the candidate's scope of work
- Which tasks I could not carry out because of it

Writing the situation precisely in English felt like a burden, so I described it to an AI to produce a draft, then checked it myself for anything inaccurate before submitting. After the exam, this kind of help is quite useful for writing a support case.

It was handled faster than I expected. **My attempt was reissued the same day.** In the [SAP certification guide](/en/blog/sap-certification) I wrote that a booking cannot be canceled or changed once confirmed, but when the cause is not the candidate's fault, such as an exam system defect, there is a way to get relief.

## Why I did not retake it right away

Right after getting the attempt back, there was an open 8 p.m. slot the same day. I could have taken it immediately, but **I passed**.

![SAP Certification Hub time selection screen showing 2 p.m., 5 p.m., and 8 p.m. slots on the same day](/images/sap-mm-c-ts452-certification-4-02.jpg)
*Figure 2. Several slots were open even on the same day. I still did not rush.*

There was one reason: **I had no confirmation that the defect had been fixed**. If the same defect remained on the same system, going back in could give the same result. There was no reason to risk wasting an attempt. Instead, I rebooked for 2 p.m. four days later, which also bought me time for one more practice run.

## What the second attempt gave me

The score was lower than the first, but in the end I lost almost nothing. I got the attempt back, and above all, **my fear of how the exam works** shrank. In summary:

1. If something is off during the exam, **don't panic; capture evidence first**
2. Check whether the problem is a setting outside your scope of work
3. Write the support case **specifically**, sticking to facts
4. Even after the attempt is reissued, **don't rush to retake without confirmation of a fix**

## Rabbit's Takeaway

When the system misbehaves during an exam, most people suspect their own mistake first, and that is the right order. But it should not end with suspicion. ==If you leave evidence that it was not your mistake, the owner of the broken kitchen takes responsibility.== I found almost no write-ups of cases like this when I searched, so I am leaving this here in the hope that it helps someone in the same situation 😎
