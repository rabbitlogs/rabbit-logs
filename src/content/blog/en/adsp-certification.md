Hi, this is Rabbit! 🐰

A head chef with years behind the stove seasons by feel. One taste from the ladle, and "that's about right." Most of the time they are right. But to pass that flavor on to a new cook, feel is not enough. You need to be able to say it in numbers: so many grams of salt, so many milliliters of water.

I was much the same with numbers on the SAP floor. "This should be fine." "It's similar to last month, so it should be okay." Whether an on-time delivery rate was at a safe level, or how much variance in production output was normal, I was ultimately judging by a gut feeling built from experience. I wanted to become someone who "reads" data rather than just "looks at" it, and the first measuring cup I picked up was the <strong class="key">ADsP</strong>.

> **Three-line summary**
> - The ADsP has 3 subjects and 50 questions; you pass with an average of 60+ across all subjects and at least 40 in each.
> - The core of my study plan was a quick pass through a condensed theory book, then repeated AI mock exams built from past questions.
> - Five AI mock exams, built from several recent rounds of reconstructed past questions, are shared at the end of this post.

## What kind of exam is the ADsP

The ADsP (Advanced Data Analytics Semi-Professional) is a nationally recognized private certification administered by the Korea Data Agency (K-DATA). It checks foundational data analysis skills, has no eligibility restrictions so anyone can take it, and once earned it stays valid with no expiry.

It is a single written exam with no practical part: 3 subjects, 50 multiple-choice questions with four options each, 100 minutes, and an exam fee of 50,000 KRW.

![Diagram of the ADsP's 3 subjects and passing criteria. Subject 1 is understanding data, Subject 2 is analytics planning, Subject 3 is data analysis; passing requires an average of 60+ across all subjects and at least 40 in each](/images/adsp-certification-01.jpg)
*Figure 1. ADsP's 3-subject structure and passing criteria (per K-DATA)*

The subjects break down like this:

- **Subject I. Understanding data**: data concepts, understanding big data, data science strategy
- **Subject II. Analytics planning**: understanding analytics planning, the analytics master plan, governance
- **Subject III. Data analysis**: R basics and data marts, statistical analysis, structured data mining

The pass mark is an **average of 60+ across all subjects**, but scoring below 40 in any one subject is an automatic fail. Subjects 1 and 2 tend to feel easier, so quite a few people neglect Subject 3 and fail on that alone.

> ⚠️ **Note**: High scores in Subjects 1 and 2 cannot make up for failing Subject 3. Secure at least 40 in Subject 3 first, then use the remaining time to raise your scores in Subjects 1 and 2.

## Two weeks, carved out of a working schedule

I studied for 2 weeks, roughly 50 hours in total. There were no big blocks of free time, so I filled it with weekday scraps and weekends.

| Time slot | Duration | How it was used |
|---|---|---|
| Weekday lunch | 30 min | Skimming the theory book, working past questions |
| Weekday evenings | 1–2 hrs | Mock exams + reviewing wrong answers |
| First weekend | 1pm–6pm | Focused, repeated mock exams |
| Final weekend | 1pm–9pm | Drilling frequently missed question types + final review |

*Table 1. A working adult's 2-week study schedule*

For the first 3–4 days I read through a condensed theory book quickly, without stopping at concepts I did not know. It is like walking into a new kitchen: before memorizing any recipe, you look around to see where the ingredients are. ==A theory book is not for understanding everything; it is for mapping which concepts show up on the exam.==

After one read-through, I added summary lectures on YouTube. Since I had already covered the theory, I played lectures under an hour at higher speed and only picked up how each concept was explained. You do not need to finish them; it is more efficient to pick only the parts that are hard to grasp from text, such as the statistics section.

## Focused training with AI mock exams

The core of my studying was past questions. ADsP question types repeat reliably from round to round, so getting used to the patterns is the fastest way through.

The past-question reconstructions I found online were less accurate than I expected. Some had wrong answers, some had odd options, some were oversimplified. Sometimes I could not tell whether I had answered wrong or the question itself was broken.

So I changed approach. I collected reconstructions from several recent rounds and asked an AI to build realistic mock exams from them. The AI organized the question types and core concepts and put together new combinations of options, so even though the questions were not identical to the originals, I could repeat the same concepts from different angles. I assumed recent rounds track the current exam most closely, so I kept the range to recent rounds.

What helped most was tracking wrong answers. I logged the numbers I missed in each round, like "round 2: missed 2, 10, 11, 20, 43", and asked the AI to find the types I kept missing and make similar questions. The more I repeated this, the more clearly I could see where I wobbled.

> 💡 **Key point**: The real value of AI mock exams is tracking wrong answers. Repeat only the ones you missed, and you quickly see which question types trip you up.

In the final week I drilled only the types I kept missing, and that was the most efficient use of time.

## After passing: a new eye for SAP data

The biggest change after passing was how I look at SAP data on the job. Exam concepts such as variance, standard deviation, regression, and cluster analysis started to show up in the numbers I see every day.

I used to look at production output and think "we made a lot this month." Now I naturally ask how far it deviates from the average and whether the daily distribution is even. I have not taken that into full analysis yet, but reaching for the measuring cup before the ladle is not a small change.

## Rabbit's Takeaway

The ADsP is not a hard exam. But if you walk in unprepared, Subject 3's statistics section trips up more people than you would expect.

Here is what I took away. ==Do not spend your time "understanding" the theory book; spend it getting used to the "patterns" of past questions.== When I first learned SAP, I also improved faster by running transactions on a real screen than by trying to memorize the manual. An exam, in the end, is about getting the shape of the questions into your hands. I hope the AI mock exams help 😎
